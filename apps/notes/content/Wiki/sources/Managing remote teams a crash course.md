# Managing remote teams a crash course

## Takeaways

- Remote setups differ: **satellite offices**, **remote employees** (minority remote), **fully distributed**, and **remote-first** (distributed + optional non-critical office) — only the last two count as truly remote.
- Hybrid setups (most in office, few remote) create asymmetric communication failures — remote-first process design matters more than "allowing WFH."
- Remote teams need **async-first communication**, explicit documentation, and intentional overlap windows — not accidental office habits stretched globally.
- Management load shifts toward **clarity of goals, written culture, and trust**; "did you see my message?" is a process failure signal.
- Useful for deciding **when remote works** (autonomous work, strong writers) vs when co-location still wins (high ambiguity, early team formation).
- Andreas Klinger's Product Hunt experience is the anchor example — fully distributed at scale.
